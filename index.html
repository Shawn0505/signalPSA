THE PROBLEM

Limitations of Current PSA Testing

Today’s prostate cancer screening relies heavily on blood-draw PSA measurements done episodically. This can delay detection, miss fast-moving changes, and generate uncertainty for patients and clinicians.

Invasive Blood Draws

Needles and clinic visits reduce screening frequency and patient adherence.

Episodic Screening

Single time‑points miss dynamic biomarker changes between appointments.

Delayed Detection

Signals may emerge months earlier in urine but go unseen without continuous monitoring.

False Positives/Negatives

PSA alone can be noisy; contextual, longitudinal analysis can improve confidence.

HOW IT WORKS

Urine Sample → Microfluidic Cartridge → Biomarker Analysis → AI Signal Processing → Longitudinal Risk Monitoring

A portable device concentrates urinary biomarkers on a microfluidic chip, detects PSA, PCA3 and related signals via biosensors, and streams data to an on‑device/secure cloud AI for anomaly detection and trend analysis.

Urine Sample

Non‑invasive collection with sterile cup or direct-to-strip adapter.

Microfluidic Cartridge

Chip separates plasma-like fractions and concentrates targets for detection.

Biomarker Analysis

PSA/PCA3 capture probes with electrochemical readout and amplification.

AI Signal Processing

Noise reduction, drift correction, multi-biomarker fusion, anomaly scoring.

Longitudinal Monitoring

Personalized baselines, trend velocity, doubling-time, and risk trajectories.
